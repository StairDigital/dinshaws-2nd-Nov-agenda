Top 10 Insights

The visit confirmed a clear entry point in drinkable and high-protein yoghurt, and that the requirements list for the line is largely ready.

  1. Drinkable is the white spaceMilky Mist has no drinkable line and Epigamia's is small. Lassi is the mass-market route in; drinkable yoghurt is the premium layer.
  2. Protein is the global premium driverValio, Lindahls and Danone lead with 25 to 26 g per pack; the case is stronger in India's vegetarian diets.
  3. India's yoghurt journey runs in reverseGlobally plain moves to drinkable, then spoonable; India starts with set dahi and needs its own consumer read.
  4. Naming drives adoptionProbiotic curd, high-protein skyr and lassi are clearer labels than "yoghurt".
  5. Tetra Top fits the shelf-life ambitionChilled, non-aseptic, 45 to 60 days below 8°C. The real test for India is the cold chain.
  6. Design for 60 days, declare less45 days at 4°C and about 15 days at 10 to 12°C once product leaves Dinshaw's control.
  7. Inclusions carry a large premiumChilled yoghurt with pieces sells about 62% above plain in China (27 vs 16.7 RMB per litre).
  8. Size the plant for 120%Concentration rarely delivers paper yields; set capacity and protein targets together, after the process is frozen.
  9. Capex is closer to Rs 30 to 34 CrOnce building, Bactofuge and filling are included; to be validated with supplier quotes.
  10. Cheese and ice cream formats are structural growthCheddar expected to double by 2030; in ice cream, extrusion shapes are hard to copy, inclusions are not.

Board and Governance

Dinshaw's is moving from a family run operating model to a professionally managed company: a professional leadership team is being inducted, the factory is being modernised, and SAP and HRMS are being implemented. 2 documents set out how this is governed: the Board Structure and Governance Framework, and the Promoter Professional Engagement Framework.

Promoters' decisions of 3 October 2026

1 person each: Chairman, Managing Director, Executive Director Ramin and Parvez inducted as directors 2 board committees in place of 3 Quarterly Audit Committee Annual review of the commercial plan Capital allocation at fixed dates early in the year

Why a Board Now

01

Ownership and Management Separate

Once professionals run execution, the promoters need a formal forum to direct, review and hold management accountable.

02

Discipline for the Transition

Factory upgrade, SAP, HRMS and the new team all need independent oversight of budgets, timelines and risks.

03

Capital Readiness

Any future capital, lender or strategic partner will expect a functioning board and a clean governance record.

04

Succession and Continuity

A board institutionalises decisions so the business does not depend on any single individual.

05

Objectivity

Independent members bring outside perspective and reduce blind spots in a family led business.

Principle

The Promoters Keep Control

The board improves the quality of that control. The board governs; the Managing Director manages.

How the Board Evolves

The people and the cadence stay the same; what changes is the board's legal standing and the formality of its process.

Today

Governance Board

Constituted by the promoters, drawing authority from their mandate. Members contribute and challenge; minutes are kept; no filings and no new statutory liability.

Year 1

Statutory Board

Effective members formally appointed with consent letters, DIN and ROC filings; notices, quorum and minutes move to the Companies Act and SS-1; the 2 committees formally constituted.

Year 3

Full Statutory Board

Independent directors designated and evaluated annually; committees run to their charters; a form a lender, investor or acquirer would accept without qualification.

2 Board Committees

Committees prepare and recommend; the board decides. Nomination, remuneration and strategy work is folded into the Annual Review Committee.

Every quarter · before the board meeting

Audit Committee

Chair: Shailesh Haribhakti · Members: Zervin Rana, Jamashp Bapuna · CFO and auditors by invitation

  • Financial reporting and MIS integrity; internal and statutory audit
  • Internal controls, SAP financial controls, delegation of authority
  • Compliance, risk register, food safety and regulatory risk
  • Capital allocation above threshold, surplus funds and treasury
  • Related party transactions; at least 1 session a year with auditors alone
Once a year · the month before the annual plan

Annual Review Committee

Chair: Jamashp Bapuna · Members: Shubham Saraff, Ramin, Parvez and the 3 experts · MD, CFO and CHRO by invitation

  • Annual review of the commercial plan, budgets, profit plans and OKRs authored by the professionals
  • Appointment, evaluation and remuneration of the professional team
  • Succession planning for critical roles
  • Board composition, evaluation and refresh; governance routines

Capital Allocation

Allocated once, early in the year, on plans the professionals wrote

  • 2 fixed dates: the Audit Committee's first meeting of the year and its second quarterly meeting; outside them, only an escalation reopens an allocation.
  • Prudent leverage: freezers from internal accruals, the factory from term debt, working capital lines never used for long term assets.
  • Who decides: CFO consolidates, Audit Committee recommends, board decides, MD executes within the envelope.

How the Board Works

The standard the board holds itself to

  • Cadence: 1 annual board meeting and 1 quarterly meeting each quarter.
  • Papers: CFO MIS pack, plan vs actuals, programme status, people, risk; circulated 7 days before.
  • Agenda: decisions first, performance second, information last.
  • Minutes: by the Company Secretary within 7 days, with an action log.
  • Access: independent directors may meet management or visit the plant without promoter presence.

Promoter Professional Engagement Framework

Professionals write their own plans, and are held to them.

7 incoming positions: CHRO, CFO, Business Head - Ice Cream, Business Head - Dairy, Sales Head - Bakery & Namkeen, Channel / Commercial Head and Head - IT. The aim is professional led execution with promoter led governance and strategy.

What Each Professional Authors

Business planBudgetProfit planTravel planExpense planHeadcount planOKRs
  • Read and test, do not rewrite: the MD and Executive Director interrogate assumptions, not numbers.
  • Approve once, then hold to it; stop approving line items the plan already covers.
  • Record disagreement, then let the plan stand, within policy and risk limits.

The Planning Cycle

  • At induction: plans drafted in month 1, approved in month 2.
  • Monthly: actuals vs plan, variances explained by the plan's author, chaired by the MD.
  • Quarterly: performance and OKRs with the board, after the Audit Committee.
  • Annually: a full re-plan, tested by the Annual Review Committee and approved by the board.
  • Roles: defined before joining, reviewed every year, reset completely every 3 years.

Engagement Steps Down

Held by the Managing Director with the Executive Director present. Each step happens only when the phase before has run to plan.

Month 1
Twice a Week

1 hour each. Walk each professional through history, customers, channels, plants and people; full access in week 1; plans drafted.

Month 2
Once a Week

1 hour. Plans reviewed and approved; promoters step out of routine operational meetings and calls.

Month 3 onwards
Fortnightly

1 hour, permanent. Decisions only at sessions or on escalation; reverse escalation enforced.

Decision Rights

Professional decides and informsAnything within approved budget, plan and authority; reported through the operating session and monthly MIS, not sought in advance.
Professional recommends, MD decidesOutside budget or plan but within the MD's delegation; structure changes below the professional level; commitments beyond the professional's authority.
MD recommends, board decidesOutside the MD's delegation; pricing architecture; leadership structure; capex above threshold; borrowing; reputational, legal or regulatory exposure.
Reverse escalationChairman, MD and Executive Director redirect operational requests back to the accountable professional: the single most important behaviour for the transition.
